SoulMate
a calm companion

UI / UX Case Study · HCI Design Rationale

A warm, non-clinical space for everyday emotional reflection

A human–computer interaction redesign of SoulMate — an agentic AI companion. The brief: reduce cognitive load, make emotional check-ins low-friction, give users real control over memory and personalisation, keep the AI explainable, support without fostering dependency, stay clearly non-clinical, and route gently toward human help when it matters.

01 — Design direction

From a clinical demo to a quiet, trustworthy room

The previous build exposed model internals (DistilBERT scores, thresholds, hardware setup) and used a cool, high-contrast “tech” blue. The redesign trades that for a space that feels like a calm room you can exhale in.

Visual style

“Warm stillness.” Soft paper backgrounds, generous whitespace, gentle 24px radii, near-invisible shadows, and a single quiet breathing motif. No gamification, no clinical white, no dashboards-of-numbers. Color is used as a feeling, not a signal.

Typography

Newsreader — a soft literary serif — for headings and the companion’s voice, lending warmth and a journaling, human quality. Hanken Grotesk for UI and body: clean, friendly, highly legible. Body never below 15.5px.

Tone & microcopy

Calm, grounded, and permission-giving. Short sentences. Always offers an out (“Maybe later”, “nothing at all”). Never diagnoses, never pressures, never performs urgency. Speaks with the user, not at them.

Layout principles

One primary action per screen. A persistent, calm left rail for orientation. Content centered in comfortable measure (≤720px reading width). Emotionally heavy moments get more space, fewer choices.

Scope — honest about what's built

What is real, prototyped, and proposed

As a final-year project, SoulMate is honest about its boundaries. Every screen carries one of three labels — also shown as a live badge in the top-right of the prototype.

A · Implemented core

Working in the actual codebase (FastAPI + WebSocket backend, React frontend): the AI companion chat, emotion & OCEAN inference, the Empty Chair technique, and safety-aware crisis routing.

B · High-fidelity prototype

Designed and clickable here to demonstrate the intended experience, but not yet wired to the backend: Today, mood check-in, reflection journal, the memory-control surface, and the dedicated safety screen.

C · Future work

Shown only as clearly-labelled proposals: data export, account deletion, anonymised model improvement, notification reminders, and any habit/goal tracking (deliberately out of scope).

03 — Key user flows

How people move through it

First-time onboarding

Welcome → what SoulMate is & isn’t (non-clinical disclaimer) → consent & memory choices → name → what brings you here → presence & boundaries → step in. (A conversational variant tells the same story as a chat.)

Daily mood check-in

One tap from Today → pick a “weather” → optionally add a few words → SoulMate reflects it back warmly → choose to talk or close. 30 seconds, skippable at every step.

Chatting with the companion

Open Companion → type or speak → replies arrive with an optional “Why this reply?” → memory recall surfaces in plain language → heavy language triggers gentle in-context safety.

Reviewing reflections

Reflections → browse a calm card wall of past moments, tagged by mood and source → open or write a new entry.

Managing memory & personalisation

Memory → see grouped, plain-language items → toggle off to forget, delete to remove for good, or clear everything → mirrored controls in Settings.

Safety routing

Risk language detected → SoulMate pauses gently, names care over task, offers human crisis lines + grounding → a persistent, non-dismissible support bar remains for the session.

08 — For your report

A paragraph you can quote

The SoulMate interface was redesigned around a single principle: an emotional-support tool should feel like a calm, trustworthy room rather than a clinical instrument. Every decision serves established HCI heuristics — progressive disclosure and a single primary action per screen reduce cognitive load; recognition-based mood input and plain-language explainability lower the friction of expressing and understanding feelings; and direct, reversible control over memory and personalisation makes the system’s behaviour transparent and keeps the user firmly in charge of their own data. A warm sand-and-sage palette, soft serif typography, and generous whitespace replace the previous high-contrast “technical” aesthetic, deliberately signalling that SoulMate is non-clinical. Crucially, the design resists fostering dependency: it favours gentle intentions over gamified streaks and actively nudges users back toward real-world connection, while an always-reachable, calmly-presented safety layer routes anyone in crisis toward trained human support. The result is an interface that is emotionally comfortable, accessible, and honest about being a companion — not a substitute for professional care.
